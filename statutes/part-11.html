21 CFR Part 11: electronic records and signatures

Binding regulationIn force since 1997 (current text, Oct 13, 2026)Applies to records FDA requires

In plain language

Part 11 sets the conditions under which FDA treats electronic records and electronic signatures as trustworthy and equivalent to paper. It covers records that FDA's rules require and records submitted to FDA.

What it requires

Controls on the system

Procedures and controls to ensure the authenticity, integrity and, when appropriate, confidentiality of records (§11.10). They include system validation, limiting access to authorized individuals, and secure, time-stamped audit trails.

More for open systems

Open systems need those controls plus additional measures “such as document encryption and use of appropriate digital signature standards” (§11.30).

Open to inspection

Computer systems, controls and documentation maintained under the part are “readily available for, and subject to, FDA inspection” (§11.1(e)).

What it does not cover

Part 11 protects the record and does not reach the sensor that produced it. It applies to records that FDA's rules require or that are submitted to FDA, so it does not by itself reach every tool in a trial. It names no specific technical security standard, and its controls are described by what they must achieve.

Transatlantic comparison

ICH E6(R3) aligns. Both expect trustworthy electronic data and access controls.
GDPR diverges. GDPR covers any personal data, including data outside the records FDA requires.

Margin notes

What “closed” and “open” mean

A closed system is one where access is controlled by the people responsible for the records. An open system is not. Open systems get extra measures.
