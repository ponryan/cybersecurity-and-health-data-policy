Sources and methodology

This page says how the dashboard values were set, what was read and what was not, and lists every source.

How the dashboard values were set

Each instrument is judged on three questions. Is it binding? Does it create legal duties, or is it guidance. Does it reach an unclassified trial wearable? A consumer-grade sensor never cleared as a medical device. Does it cover cross-border transfer? Does it address sending trial data between the U.S. and the EU.

Each answer is one of four symbols: covers it, partly or unsettled, a gap, or outside what the instrument is for. “Unsettled” means the text does not answer the question, and the answer may be yes or no.

Values come from the text of the instrument, or from an FDA or EU document that quotes or explains it. Where a statement is my own inference, it is labeled “my reading” or “author's reading.” Quoted text is copied from the source.

The scenario on the pathway page is illustrative. It is not a real trial.

Limits

  • Incident and market sources. The breach statistics and market figures come from press and market-research sources. The Novo Nordisk and Amgen incidents also draw on the companies' own statements. The DM Clinical Research database owner is not confirmed.
  • No count of unregulated trial tools. I found no study that counts how many decentralized-trial tools sit outside device regulation, which is a research gap.
  • Not legal advice. This is a policy reference for a course. It reads statutes and guidance at the level needed for the argument and does not replace advice on a specific trial.
